Hi Jo,

This isn't a job application - it's a proposal that your client might find commercially interesting.

I noticed they're investigating publication ethics at scale. I'd like to suggest that the same process could simultaneously build something valuable: structured knowledge graphs that map how scientific claims connect from first principles.

The logic: every investigation asks "does this data fit?" That question requires understanding what the established knowledge structure actually is. If you're mapping that anyway, you're creating an asset - verified semantic links between claims, with provenance tracking, that expose where gaps exist and where new papers don't connect to established foundations.

My background: PhD in Physical Biochemistry at the National Institute for Medical Research under David Trentham FRS, then 10+ years building systems in EdTech (endorsed by Toby Fotherby at AWS). I've developed a methodology called CHISG that scores claims by triangulation - how many independent sources and logical pathways support them - rather than surface-level detection.  It provides a method to protect research from AI hallucinations.

I think there's a joint project here where your client's infrastructure and access, combined with this approach, could produce knowledge graphs that are saleable in their own right - to researchers, to AI companies, to other publishers.

Happy to share more detail if this resonates with anyone on the publishing side.
