## Three Killer Opening Stories

### Story 1: ESP Behaviour Lite - From Insight to Execution

**Why Tell It**: Demonstrates you translate psychology into working products.

"I built a behavioral management system based on a simple insight: teachers operate spatially. So instead of complex forms, I created a spatial interface - a seating chart where teachers flick their finger to record behaviors. This allowed them to manage the classroom while maintaining eye contact. The data fed a live leaderboard, turning peer pressure into a positive, competitive force. It proved that great EdTech isn't about features; it's about understanding how real classrooms work."

### Story 2: Distraction Jujutsu - Channeling vs Suppressing

**Why Tell It**: Shows psychological sophistication and practical application.

"Most teachers suppress off-topic questions. I learned to channel them. When a student asked something unrelated, instead of 'Get back to work,' I'd say: 'That's a great question! [brief answer]. Let's chat more at break.' The student feels heard, and the class momentum is maintained. This principle scales to the entire platform: Don't fight student energy, channel it."

### Story 3: The Skills Map as Connective Tissue

**Why Tell It**: Your core strategic insight that solves fragmentation.

"Teachers already teach skills AND content. They just do it unconsciously. The innovation isn't adding complexity; it's making explicit what's already happening. The skills map is the backbone that connects assessment, behavior tracking, and curriculum planning into one system. Most EdTech adds more silos; this finally connects them."
