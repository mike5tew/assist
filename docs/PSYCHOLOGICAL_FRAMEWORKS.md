# Psychological Frameworks in Educational AI

This document outlines the core psychological principles that underpin the HumanOS and CHISG educational ecosystem. These frameworks translate proven pedagogical intuition into scalable technical implementations.

## 1. Voltage Regulation (Yerkes-Dodson Law)
- **Psychological Basis**: Optimal anxiety/arousal for learning. Performance peaks with a moderate level of stress but drops off if a student is either bored (too little stress) or overwhelmed (too much).
- **Teacher Struggle**: "My students are either bored or completely overwhelmed."
- **AI Implementation**: The system dynamically adjusts difficulty and support based on real-time engagement metrics (e.g., response time, error rate, self-correction). If engagement drops, it can simplify a problem; if a student is coasting, it can introduce a novel challenge.

## 2. Distraction Jujutsu (Energy Channeling)
- **Psychological Basis**: Channeling a student's energy is more effective than suppressing it. Off-topic questions are a bid for engagement, not just a distraction.
- **Teacher Struggle**: "Off-topic questions are derailing my lesson."
- **AI Implementation**: The AI follows a "Validate → Briefly Engage → Redirect → Save for Later" protocol. It acknowledges the student's curiosity, provides a brief, relevant answer, links it back to the topic, and saves the deeper question for a more appropriate time. This respects the student's engagement while maintaining focus.

## 3. Misunderstanding Mapping (Mental Model Diagnosis)
- **Psychological Basis**: Incorrect answers are not random; they are the logical output of a flawed mental model. The goal is to diagnose and fix the model, not just correct the answer.
- **Teacher Struggle**: "Why do they keep making the same mistake?"
- **AI Implementation**: The system maintains a database of common wrong answers and maps them to underlying misconceptions. When a student makes a known error, the AI doesn't just say "wrong"; it explains the flawed logic and provides the correct model.

## 4. Competence Satisfaction Engine (Self-Determination Theory)
- **Psychological Basis**: A core human need is to feel competent and effective. Small, repeated feelings of mastery build confidence and intrinsic motivation.
- **Teacher Struggle**: "How do I build confidence in my struggling students?"
- **AI Implementation**: The system is designed to detect and disproportionately celebrate "micro-wins." This could be correcting a mistake, persisting through a tough problem, or successfully using a new strategy. The feedback is specific: "You improved because you used spaced repetition," not just "Good job!"

## 5. Metalearning Feedback (Building Autonomy)
- **Psychological Basis**: Students become autonomous learners when they understand *how* they learn best (metacognition).
- **Teacher Struggle**: "My students don't know how to study effectively."
- **AI Implementation**: The AI provides feedback that connects outcomes to student actions. For example: "Your score improved 15% when you used the self-testing feature instead of just re-reading." This builds the student's awareness of their own effective learning strategies.

## 6. Social Learning Leverage (Peer Influence)
- **Psychological Basis**: Social learning theory states that people learn from one another via observation, imitation, and modeling. Peer influence is often stronger than authority.
- **Teacher Struggle**: "How do I get the whole class engaged, not just the keen students?"
- **AI Implementation**: The system facilitates healthy competition and collaboration through virtual study groups, team-based challenges, and shared progress visualizations. This channels social pressure from a negative (fear of looking stupid) to a positive (desire to help the team).
